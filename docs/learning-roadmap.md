# Learning roadmap

Order matters: each stage uses ideas from the one before. Hours are rough estimates for a first pass and depend on your background.

| Stage | Folder | Notebooks | Estimated hours | You should be able to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `00_fundamentals` | 8 | 12 to 20 | Use NumPy and pandas, explain bias and variance, split data without leakage |
| 1 | `01_supervised_learning` | 20 (8 regression, 12 classification) | 25 to 40 | Choose a metric, fit and compare models, read a learning curve |
| 2 | `02_unsupervised_learning` | 10 | 12 to 20 | Cluster, reduce dimensions, judge results without true labels |
| 3 | `03_reinforcement_learning` | 10 | 15 to 25 | Explain value functions, Bellman equations, compare on-policy and off-policy |
| 4 | `04_advanced_machine_learning` | 6 | 8 to 12 | Handle imbalance, calibrate, select features, explain, test robustness |
| 5 | `05_end_to_end_projects` | 10 | 15 to 25 | Carry a project from problem statement to recommendation |
| 6 | `06_model_deployment` | scripts and API | 4 to 8 | Save a model, serve it, monitor drift |

**Fact vs opinion.** The hour estimates are the maintainer's judgement, not measured.

## Rule of thumb for each notebook

1. Read sections 1 to 3 (problem, why this algorithm, paper).
2. Predict the result before you run the training cell.
3. Run, then do the exercises in section 12 without looking at the answer.
